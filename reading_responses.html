Reading Reflection Response 1

With the rise of social media and the use of digital cameras, many people are motivated now more than ever to capture moments through a camera. People like to take pictures of things they find appealing, like a beautiful sunset, pets, friends, etc, in order to remember those wonderful moments or to share them with others. For me, I enjoy taking pictures of things that bring me joy, appeal to me, or something I find funny. As a cat person, I love taking pictures of my cats even if they are just standing there doing nothing because they are the things that bringme joy. These current pictures will soon become distinct memories once my cats pass on, so I try to capture as many moments as possible before that happens. Photography should be cherished because images work as physical copies of memories that can hold so many emotions and could bring you back to that experience. Even when I look back at an old picture, I can remember where I was, what I was doing, and how I felt at that exact moment. In this way, photography is more than just taking a picture of something that looks nice. It can preserve a moment that would otherwise eventually be forgotten. I also think that photography allows people to share their personal experiences with others. A single photograph can show people something they may have never experienced themselves, while also giving the photographer a way to express what they find meaningful or important. You could visually represent the current world's notions through images or illustrations by taking photos representing different social issues present in the modern world. Instead of simply capturing something because it looks appealing, an artist can use an image to communicate a deeper message about society and the experiences of different communities. One painting I thought of that's relatable to many people was a painting, Because I love you, created by an artist named Lex Marie. Marie created a painting where she painted one side of a belt and then whipped the canvas with the belt. Her painting was relatable to many because people from the Black and Brown communities understood the pain of being disciplined with belt whippings from their parents. This brought attention to the generational parenting style used to instill discipline in children. Many parents did this because they believed they loved their kids and wanted the best for them, hence the title of the painting. The painting can represent how something considered normal or acceptable by one generation can become something that a younger generation questions. This brought Black and Brown communities together and created discussions among younger people wanting to break that generational cycle with their future children, so they would not have to face what their parents faced. This shows how photography and illustration can represent our current moment by gaining attention to experiences, problems, and conversations happening in society today. Images don't always have to be beautiful or happy. Sometimes, the most meaningful images are the ones that make people think, remember, or start a conversation.

Reading Reflection Response 2

This study compared groups who wrote essays without tools, used a search engine, or used an LLM such as ChatGPT. The research team found differences in brain connectivity and thinking between the groups, with the Brain-only group showing the strongest overall connectivity and in other thinking patterns. However, the LLM group underperformed in those areas, specifically in their ability to quote their own essays. This reading made me think about how I personally use AI for schoolwork. For example, I have used ChatGPT to explain difficult concepts, brainstorming ideas, or practice exam problems, but there is a difference between using it as a tool and depending on it to think for me. If I rely on AI too much, I might finish an assignment faster without actually learning or remembering the material. However, this is my experience with AI. I think this study represents only a small fraction of this topic because it was a small, preliminary study (stated multiple times) with several limitations. I feel that AI can be considered as a resource to support learning rather than replace the process of thinking and writing for oneself. While reading the article, another thing stood out. It was the response to one of the FAQ's titled, "Are you planning any additional studies in the near future?" The team wants to test the neuro impact of vibe-coding. As someone who's majoring in computer science, I have met people who have vibe-coded before, have used AI as a tool, or don't use AI because the point of being a computer science major is to do the work yourself and ask for help, not to AI. Personally, I have used AI as a tool to help me study, understand technical terms, and easily explain concepts I had a challenging time grasping, especially as someone who is shy or didn't have time to ask the professor or TAs. There is definitely a proper way to use AI as an advantage without it giving you the answer directly. As for figuring out the code, I would just do my interpretation of the coding assignment, even if I don't know; at least I'm thinking for myself first without having to rely heavily on AI. Then, I would upload the code to ChatGPT to check for errors; it would make the necessary revisions and include them in my code. I would include comments alongside those corrections, so I can understand what those lines do in my own words as a way to get into the habit of recognizing patterns for certain types of problems. There have been instances of professors encouraging AI as a tool to ask questions, to study, or to ask for feedback on papers. I feel that this FAQ will demonstrate to others how AI can be used as a resource without having to heavily rely on it, which can impact people's coding skills. Right now, there are several AI tools being promoted by several companies in order to ease the workload, but I feel that these tools should be used to help us, not do the whole thinking process for us.
